📦 How MOQ Works and How to Negotiate It

By Free sourcing by RoyblingE90 — free parts sourcing, no gatekeeping.

⚡ The short answer

MOQ (minimum order quantity) is the smallest order a factory will run — setup costs make one-offs uneconomical. To get a single unit, ask for a “sample order” at a higher per-unit price, offer roughly 30% over bulk price for one piece, bundle items from the same supplier, or ask about leftover warehouse stock from a previous run.

Why MOQ exists

Factories set up tooling, materials, and labor per production run. Making one wheel costs them nearly as much setup as making fifty, so they set a minimum order quantity to make the run worth it. It's economics, not rudeness.

How to get a single unit anyway

  • Ask for a sample order. Many suppliers sell samples at a higher per-unit price. Say the word "sample" — it's the industry's understood path to a one-off.
  • Offer to pay more per unit. "I'll pay 30% over your MOQ price for one unit" works more often than begging for the bulk price.
  • Bundle items. Need wheels and lug nuts? Ordering both from one supplier can push you over their effective minimum.
  • Ask about stock. If the part is already in their warehouse from a previous run, MOQ often disappears entirely.

What not to do

Don't promise a "big order later" you can't back up — sellers hear it daily and price you accordingly. And don't let a low MOQ blind you to a bad supplier; one unit from a scammer is still a loss.

Quick answers

What is MOQ on Alibaba?

Minimum Order Quantity — the smallest order a factory will produce. Setup costs make tiny runs uneconomical, so factories set a minimum to make production worthwhile.

Can I buy just one item on Alibaba?

Often, yes. Ask for a “sample order” at a higher per-unit price, offer roughly 30% over the bulk price for one unit, bundle items from the same supplier, or ask if they have leftover warehouse stock.

How do I negotiate MOQ with a supplier?

Lead with “sample order” — it's the industry's understood path to a one-off. Offering a fair per-unit premium works far better than promising a big future order you can't back up.
