How to Spot a Scam Supplier (Red Flags Checklist)

By Free sourcing by RoyblingE90 — free parts sourcing, no gatekeeping.

The short answer

Walk away from any Alibaba supplier that refuses Trade Assurance or pushes payment off the platform — those two flags are disqualifying on their own. Also watch for zero transaction history, prices far below market, stock photos only, no verifiable business info, and pressure tactics. There is always another supplier.

Run through this before paying anything

  • No transaction history. Zero completed orders and a brand-new storefront is the biggest single red flag.
  • Prices far below market. If every forged wheel is $4,000 and one seller offers them for $400, you're not getting a deal — you're getting a lesson.
  • Refuses Trade Assurance. A legitimate supplier has no reason to dodge payment protection. This one alone is disqualifying.
  • Pushes payment off Alibaba. "Wire me directly," "pay on WhatsApp," "crypto only" — every one of these means zero protection.
  • Stock photos only. Can't produce a real photo or video of the actual product, the factory, or packaging when asked.
  • No verifiable business info. Vague company name, no address, no business license when asked.
  • Pressure tactics. "Price goes up tomorrow," "only one left at this price" — urgency is a sales tool, but paired with other flags it's a trap.
  • Copy-paste answers. Doesn't answer your specific fitment questions, just repeats the listing text.

The golden rule

Any ONE of the payment-related flags — no Trade Assurance, off-platform payment — is enough to walk away, no matter how good the price looks. There is always another supplier.

Quick answers

How can I tell if an Alibaba supplier is a scam?

Biggest red flags: no transaction history, prices far below market, refusal of Trade Assurance, and pushing payment off Alibaba. Any payment-related flag alone is reason to walk away.

Is it safe to pay a supplier outside Alibaba?

No. Off-platform payment — wire transfer, WhatsApp, crypto — means zero protection. Legitimate suppliers have no reason to dodge Trade Assurance.

What should I do if a supplier's price seems too good to be true?

Walk away. If every forged wheel is $4,000 and one seller offers $400, you're not getting a deal. Compare against market prices and check transaction history.
