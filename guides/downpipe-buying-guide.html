🔥 Downpipe Buying Guide: Catted vs Catless, Sizing, and Fitment

By Free sourcing by RoyblingE90 — free parts sourcing, no gatekeeping.

⚡ The short answer

For most street cars, buy a catted downpipe: nearly all the power gain of catless with far less smell and a better chance of staying emissions-legal. Match diameter to your power goals (3 inches suits most stock-turbo cars), insist on a proper braided flex section, and budget for a tune — that's the real fix for the check-engine light. Confirm engine code and drivetrain with the seller.

What a downpipe does

The downpipe is the first section of exhaust after the turbo. The factory one is restrictive — tight bends, a dense catalytic converter — because it was designed for emissions and noise rules, not flow. A freer-flowing downpipe is usually the single best bang-for-buck power mod on a turbo car, especially with a tune to take advantage of it.

Catted vs catless: the honest version

Catless: maximum flow, maximum power, maximum smell, guaranteed check-engine light without a tune, and illegal for street use in most places with emissions testing. High-flow catted: keeps a less-restrictive catalytic converter — nearly all the power gain, far less smell, and a much better chance of staying emissions-legal. Our take: unless it's a dedicated track car, buy catted. The 5–10hp difference isn't worth the daily downsides.

Sizing: bigger is not automatically better

Match the diameter to your power goals. For most stock-turbo cars, 3" (76mm) is the sweet spot — going to 3.5"+ on a stock turbo can actually lose low-end response with zero top-end gain to show for it. Big-turbo builds are a different story. If the listing doesn't state the diameter clearly, ask — "fits your car" is not a spec.

The flex section matters more than you think

Your engine moves on its mounts; your exhaust doesn't. The flex section (the braided part) absorbs that movement. Cheap downpipes use thin flex sections that crack — then you're chasing an exhaust leak and a CEL. Look for a proper braided flex section with good welds all around. This is the #1 quality difference between a $150 pipe and a $400 pipe.

The check-engine light question

Any downpipe change can trigger a CEL for catalyst efficiency. Catted pipes with a quality converter often avoid it; catless almost always triggers it. The proper fix is a tune — O2 spacers/defoulers are a band-aid that sometimes works and sometimes doesn't. Budget the tune into the real cost of the mod.

What to confirm before ordering

Downpipes are engine- and chassis-specific: send the seller your exact year, model, engine code, and whether the car is FWD/AWD (it changes the routing). Ask about the flange type, whether new gaskets and hardware are included, and the catalytic converter cell count if catted.
