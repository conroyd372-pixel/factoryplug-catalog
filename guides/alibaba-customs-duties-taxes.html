The short answer

Whether you pay import duty on an Alibaba order depends on three things: your country's de minimis threshold (the value under which imports enter duty-free), the shipping terms you agreed (DDP vs DAP), and the product's tariff classification. Most small parts orders from individual buyers slide under de minimis and arrive with no extra charges. Big orders don't.

De minimis: the threshold that matters most

Many countries let low-value imports in duty-free — for the US this has historically been $800 per shipment, though trade rules change and you should check the current threshold before a big order. If your order's declared value is under the threshold, there's typically nothing extra to pay. Above it, duties and processing fees apply. Never ask a seller to under-declare value — that's customs fraud, and it voids your Trade Assurance protection if the shipment is seized.

DDP vs DAP: who pays the duty

DDP (Delivered Duty Paid) means the seller handles customs and duty — the price you're quoted is the price you pay, full stop. DAP (Delivered At Place) means YOU are the importer: the carrier will bill you for duties, taxes, and a brokerage fee before they'll release the package. DDP almost always works out cheaper and simpler for individual buyers. Read our full shipping terms guide for the details.

Estimating duty before you buy

Ask the seller two questions: "What HS code will you declare?" and "Is this DDP or DAP?" The HS code is the tariff classification — with it, you can look up your country's duty rate for that product category (auto parts rates vary widely by material and type). Then do the math: part price + shipping + (duty rate × declared value) + any brokerage fee = your real landed cost. Compare that against domestic prices before you commit.

What can go wrong

  • DAP surprise bills. The classic: a $400 part with a $180 carrier duty/brokerage bill on delivery. Always confirm DDP vs DAP in writing.
  • Customs holds. Random inspections happen. A legit seller's commercial invoice and your Trade Assurance order record get it released — another reason to keep everything inside Alibaba.
  • Restricted materials. Some countries restrict certain chemicals, used parts, or items with refrigerants. If your part includes fluids, batteries, or pressurized components, ask the seller about shipping restrictions first.

Keep the paperwork

Save the commercial invoice, tracking, and your Alibaba order record until the part is installed and you're happy. If anything arrives damaged or wrong, that paperwork is your evidence — see what to do when an order arrives damaged.

Quick answers

Do I have to pay customs duty on Alibaba orders?

It depends on your country's de minimis threshold, your shipping terms, and the product's tariff classification. Small orders under the threshold usually arrive with no extra charges; larger orders can attract duties and carrier brokerage fees.

What is the difference between DDP and DAP for customs?

With DDP (Delivered Duty Paid) the seller handles customs and duty — the quoted price is final. With DAP (Delivered At Place) you are the importer and the carrier bills you for duties and brokerage before releasing the package. DDP is simpler and usually cheaper for individual buyers.

Should I ask the seller to declare a lower value to avoid duty?

No. Under-declaring value is customs fraud, and it can void your Trade Assurance protection if the shipment is seized or inspected. Get the real HS code and shipping terms in writing instead, and compute your true landed cost before ordering.

How do I estimate import duty before buying?

Ask the seller for the HS tariff code they'll declare and whether shipping is DDP or DAP. Look up your country's duty rate for that code, then add part price + shipping + duty + any brokerage fee to get your real landed cost.
