Should You Use a Buying Agent or Buy Direct?

By Free sourcing by RoyblingE90 — free parts sourcing, no gatekeeping.

The short answer

Use a buying agent (typically 5–15% of order value) for your first large order, multi-supplier shipments, or when you want independent quality inspection with photos. Buy direct when you know exactly what you want from a strong supplier — most single car parts fall here. Avoid agents with no references or fees demanded fully up front.

On this page

  • When an agent makes sense
  • When to buy direct

What a buying agent does

A good agent sources the part, negotiates, inspects quality before it ships, consolidates multiple suppliers into one shipment, and handles freight. You're paying for expertise and risk reduction, typically 5–15% of the order value or a flat fee.

When an agent makes sense

  • Your first large order and you don't know the process yet.
  • You're buying from several suppliers and want one combined shipment.
  • You want independent quality inspection with photos before the container seals.
  • The order value is high enough that a 10% fee is cheaper than a total loss.

When to buy direct

If you know exactly what you want, found a strong supplier (high rating, deep transaction history), and it's a single straightforward part — going direct keeps the full margin in your pocket. Most single car parts fall in this bucket.

Avoiding bad agents

The agent world has its own scammers: no references, no written inspection reports, fees demanded entirely up front, no verifiable company. Get everything in writing, pay in stages, and never let an agent route payment to their personal account off-platform.

Quick answers

Do I need a buying agent for Alibaba?

Not for most single car parts. Use an agent (typically 5–15%) for your first large order, multi-supplier shipments, or when you want independent quality inspection with photos before it ships.

How much does an Alibaba buying agent cost?

Typically 5–15% of the order value or a flat fee. For high-value orders, that fee is cheaper than a total loss on a bad shipment.

How do I avoid a bad buying agent?

Demand references, written inspection reports with photos, and staged payments. Avoid agents with no verifiable company, no references, or fees demanded entirely up front.
